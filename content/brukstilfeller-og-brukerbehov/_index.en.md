Who needs information about education and career choices, what they need, and what the fragmented offering means for them.

## The use case

The pilot builds on [case 21 – Choice of educational pathway](/en/behov/use-cases/21-valg-utdanningsloep/).

The choice of education and occupation is among the most significant choices a person makes. Incorrect, outdated or biased information can lead to wrong choices and drop-out, longer study paths, exclusion from working life or a poorer match between society's competence needs and individual choices. When good information about how relevant the education programmes are to working life is lacking, choices are made under uncertainty.

Today there are several public digital services for education and career information: utdanning.no, vilbli.no, the question service ung.no and karriereveiledning.no. For twenty years, search engines, mainly Google, have distributed traffic between them according to which website seems most relevant. In recent years, commercial AI services have also used text from the public websites as a source for answers that are not quality-assured, and advertising-funded websites crowd out more neutral sources.

The content on vilbli.no and utdanning.no partly overlaps. Working life, occupations, training companies, further education and admission to vocational college, university and university college are found on utdanning.no, while the school and subject offering at each upper secondary school and county authority is presented in most detail on vilbli.no. Users therefore have to move between the websites, even though little has been done to make this easy.

## The users and what they need

| Who | User journey | How the pilot proposes to learn more |
|---|---|---|
| Young people in primary, lower secondary and upper secondary school | Search for upper secondary education | Continuous user insight, user testing and, for example, a pupil panel |
| Parents | Search for upper secondary education | Interviews, simple surveys and user tests |
| Adults | Search for upper secondary education | Covered through Upper secondary for adults (Videregående for voksne), with good entry points to it |
| Career counsellors, advisers and teachers | Search for upper secondary education | Co-creation with karriereveiledning.no, a reference group with staff from the county career centres, and briefings in networks and at national courses for advisers |
| Staff in the county authorities (admission, vocational training, adult education, follow-up service, examinations and career centres) | Search for upper secondary education | Interviews, simple surveys and user tests |
| Local editors in the county authorities | Present correct local information | Co-creation and a reference group with some local editors, where national and local information are seen together |
| Editors of subject offerings at the upper secondary schools | Present correct and up-to-date information | Input through a small reference group or survey |
| Kristne Friskolers Forbund (KFF), on behalf of the private schools | Present correct and up-to-date information about private schools on a shared website | Co-creation and reference group |
| Training companies and training offices | Present correct information | Input through a small reference group or survey |

## Consequences of the current situation

**For end users** (pupils, students, parents and adults):

- They receive few personalised services, often because the information is spread across several websites.
- They risk missing essential information, because the website they use lacks good information about relevance to working life.
- More people seek guidance on social media and commercial AI services instead of neutral public channels.

**For staff in the services:**

- Duplicate work updating overlapping content.
- No overall insight into user behaviour and information needs across the services.

**For the organisations:**

- Inefficient use of resources when parallel solutions deliver overlapping services.
- Fragmented expert communities and higher total operating costs.
- Unclear lines of responsibility for the quality and updating of content.
- It becomes expensive and difficult to develop good public AI services and personalised services when the data sources are not brought together.
